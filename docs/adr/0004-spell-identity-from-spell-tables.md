# Spell Identity reads spells from tables, and learns only a wand's school

Spell Identity tells which spell made Your Hit from the player's casts, the Hit's time, school and Line Kind, and for a tick the Tick Rhythm on its Nameplate; there is no link from a cast to a Nameplate. It reads the spells from two built-in tables made from Forever's spell data for the cast of every damage spell in a class or pet skill line or a talent: the spells that tick (`src/TickSpells.lua`: each spell's interval and school, the longest duration of any of its ranks with talents, whether its cast also hits, whether it travels first, and whether it is an area spell) and the spells that hit directly (`src/DirectSpells.lua`: each rank's school). A tick fits a spell one interval after the spell's cast, or after its landing on that Nameplate for one that travels and hits, then on the spell's Tick Rhythm until its duration ends; two ticks of one school at once on one Nameplate, such as Fireball's and Ignite's, get none. On Forever an area spell's ticks come as direct hits of another spell ID (seen for Rain of Fire; untested for the other area spells), so a direct hit also fits a channelled area spell whose tick is due a whole number of intervals after its cast (the first up to 1.5 s later for one that travels, such as Volley), or after its cast's last tick once one came, until its duration ends or its channel stops; if another cast also fits, or two area spells fit, the Hit gets none. The area spells that are not channels (Consecration, Flamestrike's DoT) are left out: a proc with no cast, such as a seal's, would take their icon whenever it landed on their tick grid, while during a channel the player can neither swing nor cast. A direct hit or a shot takes the one cast of the last 1.5 s that fits it, of a spell in a table whose school fits. Only casts of spells that can make that Hit compete: for a direct hit, the direct table's spells that are not ranged attacks, which also holds the pets' damage spells, and the ticking spells whose cast also hits; for a shot, the ranged attacks; for a direct miss also any ticking spell at its cast, or after its flight if it travels, as its application can miss. A pet's cast, or one whose spell ID is secret, counts against a Hit's spell but never for it; a buff's cast or a Voidwalker's Torment does not count at all, and a DoT that only ticks does not count for a hit, so none of them can make a Shadow Bolt's hit unsure, and a proc with no cast of its own, such as a poison's, gets no icon instead of the buff's.

The first plan learned these facts instead, from Hits that only one cast spell could explain. In the Warlock Traces nearly every tick had several such spells (Shoot, Shadow Bolt, curses and other DoTs come within a few seconds of each tick), so about 25 of about 313 spell Hits got an icon, and learning from every spell that could explain a tick gave wrong icons, as a steady rotation lines a wrong spell up with the ticks again and again. Learning direct hits let a buff cast learn the school of the procs that came after it, and then show its icon on them. With the tables, about 290 got one and none was wrong. A spell that is not in a table gets no icon, and a new class, patch or Forever spell needs a new table row.

Only a wand's Shoot learns its school, as a Spell Fact, because the wand sets it: a fact counts once two Hits agree on it, and a Hit that only one cast explains and that disagrees starts it again, as a new wand changes it. The player can clear it (`/nsctr forget`, or a button in the Icons options). The facts are saved in `db.global`. A Hit carries no source, so a pet's Hit with no cast seen, a proc, or a spell that is in no table can still be taken for the player's spell when that spell's cast is the only one in its window whose school fits.
