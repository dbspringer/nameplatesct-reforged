# Spell Identity reads spells from tables, and learns only a wand's school

Spell Identity tells which spell made Your Hit from the player's casts, the Hit's time, school and Line Kind, and for a tick the Tick Rhythm on its Nameplate. A cast links to a Nameplate only for its ticks, through the player's target when the cast started; a direct hit or a shot has no such link. It reads the spells from two built-in tables made from Forever's spell data for the cast of every damage spell in a class or pet skill line or a talent: the spells that tick (`src/TickSpells.lua`: each spell's interval and school, the longest duration of any of its ranks with talents, whether its cast also hits, whether it travels first, and whether it is an area spell) and the spells that hit directly (`src/DirectSpells.lua`: each rank's school). A tick fits a spell a whole number of intervals after the spell's cast on that Nameplate, or after its landing there for one that travels and hits, then a whole number of intervals after the spell's last tick there, until its duration ends; a cast with no target fits only its first tick, one interval later, on any Nameplate. A channel's tick that comes in the frame of its stop (a Stop Tick) fits the channel off its rhythm, when the channel ticked on that Nameplate less than one interval before. At a Tick Tie, each tied spell keeps every rhythm that can be its own, the old ones and the one that the tie gives it, until a tick fits it alone; an old rhythm that ran out or that a recast replaced stays as no rhythm, so that the cast can still give the spell's first tick. Two ticks of one school at once on one Nameplate, such as Fireball's and Ignite's, get none. On Forever an area spell's ticks come as direct hits of another spell ID (seen for Rain of Fire; untested for the other area spells), so a direct hit also fits a channelled area spell whose tick is due a whole number of intervals after its cast (the first up to 1.5 s later for one that travels, such as Volley), or after its cast's last tick once one came, until its duration ends or its channel stops; if another cast also fits, or two area spells fit, the Hit gets none. The area spells that are not channels (Consecration, Flamestrike's DoT) are left out: a proc with no cast, such as a seal's, would take their icon whenever it landed on their tick grid, while during a channel the player can neither swing nor cast. A direct hit or a shot takes the one cast of the last 1.5 s that fits it, of a spell in a table whose school fits. Only casts of spells that can make that Hit compete: for a direct hit, the direct table's spells that are not ranged attacks, which also holds the pets' damage spells, and the ticking spells whose cast also hits; for a shot, the ranged attacks; for a direct miss also any ticking spell at its cast, or after its flight if it travels, as its application can miss. A pet's cast, or one whose spell ID is secret, counts against a Hit's spell but never for it; a buff's cast or a Voidwalker's Torment does not count at all, and a DoT that only ticks does not count for a hit, so none of them can make a Shadow Bolt's hit unsure, and a proc with no cast of its own, such as a poison's, gets no icon instead of the buff's.

A tick used to fit only one interval after the spell's cast or its last tick, and a cast fitted ticks on any Nameplate. No spell drifts from its Tick Rhythm in the Traces, but one tick can come up to 0.2 s off and the next is back on it (`warlock-tick-jitter`, `warlock-line-kind`), so one late tick cost the spell its icon for the rest of its duration. A wider tolerance made more ties, and a tie moves the other spell's rhythm too, so the tolerance stays 0.1 s and a tick fits whole intervals instead. Whole intervals from a cast on any Nameplate fitted the ticks of the same spell on other mobs, and so did the first tick (18 wrong icons in `warlock-rfk`), so a cast counts only on its target. That target is the one at the cast's start (its SENT): at its end the player can have changed target, which in `warlock-rfk` and `warlock-bfd-group` gave 11 Immolates' ticks to the wrong mob. A cast with no start target, as in Traces from before casts recorded one, or when its SENT cannot be matched because its cast GUID is secret, keeps the old first-tick fit. A refresh replaces the older casts' ticks, and a channel's ticks end when it stops. Over the checked Traces this took Unknown from 49 to 32 with no wrong icon, and on the unchecked dungeon Traces wrong icons from 101 to 83.

Channels in `warlock-drain-life` that stopped early, at about 3.75 s (cause unknown), gave a tick in the stop's frame, off the rhythm (10 of 53 lost Drain Life icons; older Traces had none). That tick now fits the channel, but only less than one interval after the channel's last tick on that Nameplate: a Hit in the stop's frame long after it can be one whose kill stopped the channel (`warlock-rfk`). It competes like a rhythm, so another spell that also fits makes a tie, and it gets none while another DoT of its school on that Nameplate has a tick due within 0.2 s, on its rhythm's last tick or source grid, or as a tick of a cast that no rhythm holds while its DoT can run: that DoT's tick can kill the mob, which stops the channel in the same frame, a little off its own time. Curse of Doom, which the table leaves out, counts here too (one tick at 60 s; Classic IDs, unverified on Forever), and so does any cast whose spell ID is secret, for as long as any DoT can run (no Trace has one). For the same reason there is no Stop Tick until Spell Identity has run longer than any DoT (60 s): a DoT cast before it started, as before a /reload or a Trace's start, is in no list. The stop is known in time, as Flow takes a frame's casts before Pairing decides that frame's Hits. A stop marks only a cast whose channel can still run, as a cast whose stop never came stayed in a rhythm on another mob and took a later stop, which left the running channel open. Over the checked Traces this took Unknown from 279 to 270 (Drain Life's from 53 to 46 in `warlock-drain-life`) with no new wrong icon there or in the unchecked Traces.

A Tick Tie used to move every tied spell's rhythm onto the tie. Only one spell made it, so the other spell's rhythm was then off its own tick, which came 0.1 to 0.17 s later and missed; Drain Life's 1 s interval divides 2 s and 3 s, so a moved rhythm kept meeting Drain Life's ticks for the rest of the channel (16 lost Drain Life icons in `warlock-drain-life`). A first rule kept one rhythm per spell and chose which one to keep at a tie (the old one, a retry of the cast, a 0.2 s window after the tie); each review round found a timing case where it threw away the true rhythm and gave a wrong icon, so it was left out. Keeping every rhythm that can be true never loses a spell's own tick while each tick comes within the tolerance of its rhythm: a false rhythm can only add a fit, which makes a tie and no icon. The rule must keep "no rhythm yet" as well: in `warlock-rfk` (Hits 2154 and 19406) Bane of Agony's tick tied with the first tick of a new or recast Siphon Life or Corruption, whose own first tick came 0.03 s later; a spell left with only the tie's rhythm could not take its cast's first tick, and Agony's old rhythm took it alone. The limit is a tick past the tolerance: when the tie's other spell ticks 0.105 to 0.125 s off its rhythm, only the tie's owner fits that tick, and takes it (three review cases, kept as specs of the limit; no Trace has one). n ticks of one school at once on one Nameplate move every spell that fits when no more than n spells fit, as each ticked; when more fit, they count as a tie, so that a spell that sat out the frame keeps its true rhythm (a review case: Siphon Life's older kept rhythm fit a frame of Agony's and Drain Life's ticks, which moved its rhythm, and its next tick took Drain Life's icon). Keeping every rhythm in every such frame cost icons. A tick whose school is secret keeps the frame's icons away, but does not count in n, as it can be of another school (a review case: an Immolate tick with a secret school made one Shadow tick count as two, which moved both Shadow spells that fit it). A spell that still holds rhythms from a tie fits more ticks than its own, and a DoT cast whose spell ID is secret has no rhythm, so while such a cast can still run (60 s), a tick that only such a spell fits gets none and, like a tie, narrows no rhythm (review cases: a Drain Life rhythm that ties kept fit a secret Siphon Life cast's tick alone; a blocked tick that narrowed Corruption's kept rhythms let Siphon Life's next tick take Corruption's icon; no Trace has such a cast). A DoT cast before Identity started, as before a /reload, is a known limit: its tick can make a crowded frame that a kept rhythm also fits look full, which moves that rhythm (a review case). Keeping every rhythm for the first 60 s instead gave new wrong icons in `warlock-drain-life` and in replays from mid-Trace, and replays of every Trace from every 50th entry (1,608 starts) give no wrong icon that main does not, and fewer Unknown. A Stop Tick's guard reads any of the channel's rhythms, as its last tick can have been a tie, and its rival check reads all of the rival's. A channel's stop reads only the newest cast of a spell's rhythms on each Nameplate: an older cast that a tie kept belongs to a channel that ended, whose stop can have come with a secret spell ID, and it took the recast's stop in a review case. Over the checked Traces this took Unknown from 270 to 250 (Bane of Agony 11, Corruption 4, Drain Life 3, Siphon Life 2 in `warlock-drain-life`, where Drain Life's lost icons went from 46 to 43, as most of them are on the ties themselves), with no new wrong icon there or in the unchecked Traces (`warlock-open-world-2` 23 to 13, `warlock-rfk` 546 to 537, `warlock-sm-armory` 445 to 443) and no change in replays that start mid-Trace. No spell held more than 3 rhythms on distinct grids in any Trace.

The first plan learned these facts instead, from Hits that only one cast spell could explain. In the Warlock Traces nearly every tick had several such spells (Shoot, Shadow Bolt, curses and other DoTs come within a few seconds of each tick), so about 25 of about 313 spell Hits got an icon, and learning from every spell that could explain a tick gave wrong icons, as a steady rotation lines a wrong spell up with the ticks again and again. Learning direct hits let a buff cast learn the school of the procs that came after it, and then show its icon on them. With the tables, about 290 got one and none was wrong. A spell that is not in a table gets no icon, and a new class, patch or Forever spell needs a new table row.

Only a wand's Shoot learns its school, as a Spell Fact, because the wand sets it: a fact counts once two Hits agree on it, and a Hit that only one cast explains and that disagrees starts it again, as a new wand changes it. A fact that counts starts again only at the second such Hit in a row of one other school: in a group one Hit that disagrees can be another player's that took the shot's line (`warlock-rfk`: 4 of these in 55 minutes, each of which cost the wand's school for 14 s or more). The player can clear it (`/nsctr forget`, or a button in the Icons options). The facts are saved in `db.global`. A Hit carries no source, so a pet's Hit with no cast seen, a proc, or a spell that is in no table can still be taken for the player's spell when that spell's cast is the only one in its window whose school fits.

A Seal Proc is the first exception to "a proc gets no icon", and a Poison Proc the second (below). The Hit comes on a direct line with no cast of its own, so it is tied to the Seal by the player's casts and a landed swing: the player's last Seal cast is the Active Seal for 30 s, until another Seal replaces it, and the Hit takes it when it is Holy and comes within 0.15 s after the player's own swing line. A missed proc gets none: a miss has no school, and in the Traces other on-hit procs' misses (Touch of the Grave's 21, Windfury's 4) outnumber Seal of Command's 4. This is built on casts, not on the Seal's aura, as casts are public and in every Trace, so replays can test the rule; the cost is no icon after a reload until the next Seal cast. As everywhere in this ADR, an unsure Hit gets none: a cast that also fits blocks it, and so does Consecration for its 8 s, since its ticks are direct Holy Hits with no cast and about 15% of swings would line up with one, whichever comes first. Secret amounts rule out telling them apart by size. Judgement does not end a Seal on Forever, unlike Classic: in the Traces, 26 of 77 Judgements had a proc of the same Seal after them with no recast. It is an ordinary row of the direct table, though its damage is another spell, with a window of 0.2 s after its cast in place of 1.5 s, and a Hit that could be its, a Seal Proc, or a Consecration tick gets none. Only Seal of Righteousness has a Trace of the player's own. Seal of Command and Seal of Fury rest on other players' lines, and Seal of Martyrdom, which no Trace has, only replaces the Active Seal.

A Rogue's Poison Procs get an icon by school and timing alone, on a Rogue only (the player's class from `UnitClass`). Instant Poison's Hit is a Nature Hit on a direct line with no cast; it takes Instant Poison's icon when it comes within 0.15 s after one of the player's landed swing lines or Physical direct Hits (strikes): in `warlock-rfk`, 339 of a party Rogue's 341 Instant Poison II hits came 1 to 100 ms after one of their swings or strikes on the same target. Deadly Poison's ticks are Nature ticks with no cast, so a Nature tick that no Tick Rhythm fits takes Deadly Poison's icon; its stacks tick as one, so two at once still take it. Each takes its first rank's spell ID, as nothing tells the rank. The poison on each weapon is not checked, though the game names it by its enchant (`GetWeaponEnchantInfo`), because no test has shown that Forever lets an addon read it; casts would lose it at each reload, as a poison lasts 30 minutes. A Rogue has no Nature spell of its own, so the cost is a poison icon on a rare Nature proc from an item. Amounts cannot tell them apart: they are secret in every recent Trace. Reading the enchant is the fix once a `/run` test shows it is public. No Trace has a Rogue as the recorder, so the rule rests on another player's Combat Log File events and on synthetic specs.
