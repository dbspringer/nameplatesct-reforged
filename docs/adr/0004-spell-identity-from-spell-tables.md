# Spell Identity reads spells from tables, and learns only a wand's school

Spell Identity tells which spell made Your Hit from the player's casts, the Hit's time, school and Line Kind, and for a tick the Tick Rhythm on its Nameplate. A cast links to a Nameplate only for its ticks, through the player's target when the cast started; a direct hit or a shot has no such link. It reads the spells from two built-in tables made from Forever's spell data for the cast of every damage spell in a class or pet skill line or a talent: the spells that tick (`src/TickSpells.lua`: each spell's interval and school, the longest duration of any of its ranks with talents, whether its cast also hits, whether it travels first, and whether it is an area spell) and the spells that hit directly (`src/DirectSpells.lua`: each rank's school). A tick fits a spell a whole number of intervals after the spell's cast on that Nameplate, or after its landing there for one that travels and hits, then a whole number of intervals after the spell's last tick there, until its duration ends; a cast with no target fits only its first tick, one interval later, on any Nameplate. Two ticks of one school at once on one Nameplate, such as Fireball's and Ignite's, get none. On Forever an area spell's ticks come as direct hits of another spell ID (seen for Rain of Fire; untested for the other area spells), so a direct hit also fits a channelled area spell whose tick is due a whole number of intervals after its cast (the first up to 1.5 s later for one that travels, such as Volley), or after its cast's last tick once one came, until its duration ends or its channel stops; if another cast also fits, or two area spells fit, the Hit gets none. The area spells that are not channels (Consecration, Flamestrike's DoT) are left out: a proc with no cast, such as a seal's, would take their icon whenever it landed on their tick grid, while during a channel the player can neither swing nor cast. A direct hit or a shot takes the one cast of the last 1.5 s that fits it, of a spell in a table whose school fits. Only casts of spells that can make that Hit compete: for a direct hit, the direct table's spells that are not ranged attacks, which also holds the pets' damage spells, and the ticking spells whose cast also hits; for a shot, the ranged attacks; for a direct miss also any ticking spell at its cast, or after its flight if it travels, as its application can miss. A pet's cast, or one whose spell ID is secret, counts against a Hit's spell but never for it; a buff's cast or a Voidwalker's Torment does not count at all, and a DoT that only ticks does not count for a hit, so none of them can make a Shadow Bolt's hit unsure, and a proc with no cast of its own, such as a poison's, gets no icon instead of the buff's.

A tick used to fit only one interval after the spell's cast or its last tick, and a cast fitted ticks on any Nameplate. No spell drifts from its Tick Rhythm in the Traces, but one tick can come up to 0.2 s off and the next is back on it (`warlock-tick-jitter`, `warlock-line-kind`), so one late tick cost the spell its icon for the rest of its duration. A wider tolerance made more ties, and a tie moves the other spell's rhythm too, so the tolerance stays 0.1 s and a tick fits whole intervals instead. Whole intervals from a cast on any Nameplate fitted the ticks of the same spell on other mobs, and so did the first tick (18 wrong icons in `warlock-rfk`), so a cast counts only on its target. That target is the one at the cast's start (its SENT): at its end the player can have changed target, which in `warlock-rfk` and `warlock-bfd-group` gave 11 Immolates' ticks to the wrong mob. Traces from before casts recorded a target keep the old first-tick fit. Over the checked Traces this took Unknown from 49 to 32 with no wrong icon, and on the unchecked dungeon Traces wrong icons from 101 to 83.

The first plan learned these facts instead, from Hits that only one cast spell could explain. In the Warlock Traces nearly every tick had several such spells (Shoot, Shadow Bolt, curses and other DoTs come within a few seconds of each tick), so about 25 of about 313 spell Hits got an icon, and learning from every spell that could explain a tick gave wrong icons, as a steady rotation lines a wrong spell up with the ticks again and again. Learning direct hits let a buff cast learn the school of the procs that came after it, and then show its icon on them. With the tables, about 290 got one and none was wrong. A spell that is not in a table gets no icon, and a new class, patch or Forever spell needs a new table row.

Only a wand's Shoot learns its school, as a Spell Fact, because the wand sets it: a fact counts once two Hits agree on it, and a Hit that only one cast explains and that disagrees starts it again, as a new wand changes it. A fact that counts starts again only at the second such Hit in a row of one other school: in a group one Hit that disagrees can be another player's that took the shot's line (`warlock-rfk`: 4 of these in 55 minutes, each of which cost the wand's school for 14 s or more). The player can clear it (`/nsctr forget`, or a button in the Icons options). The facts are saved in `db.global`. A Hit carries no source, so a pet's Hit with no cast seen, a proc, or a spell that is in no table can still be taken for the player's spell when that spell's cast is the only one in its window whose school fits.

A Seal Proc is the one exception to "a proc gets no icon". The Hit comes on a direct line with no cast of its own, so it is tied to the Seal by the player's casts and a landed swing: the player's last Seal cast is the Active Seal for 30 s, until another Seal replaces it, and the Hit takes it when it is Holy and comes within 0.15 s after the player's own swing line. A missed proc gets none: a miss has no school, and in the Traces other on-hit procs' misses (Touch of the Grave's 21, Windfury's 4) outnumber Seal of Command's 4. This is built on casts, not on the Seal's aura, as casts are public and in every Trace, so replays can test the rule; the cost is no icon after a reload until the next Seal cast. As everywhere in this ADR, an unsure Hit gets none: a cast that also fits blocks it, and so does Consecration for its 8 s, since its ticks are direct Holy Hits with no cast and about 15% of swings would line up with one, whichever comes first. Secret amounts rule out telling them apart by size. Judgement does not end a Seal on Forever, unlike Classic: in the Traces, 26 of 77 Judgements had a proc of the same Seal after them with no recast. It is an ordinary row of the direct table, though its damage is another spell, with a window of 0.2 s after its cast in place of 1.5 s, and a Hit that could be its, a Seal Proc, or a Consecration tick gets none. Only Seal of Righteousness has a Trace of the player's own. Seal of Command and Seal of Fury rest on other players' lines, and Seal of Martyrdom, which no Trace has, only replaces the Active Seal.
